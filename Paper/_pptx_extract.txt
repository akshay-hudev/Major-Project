INTRODUCTION
• Cardiovascular deterioration is a trajectory, not a single snapshot.
• We formulate progression as binary classification: worsening vs stable/improving.
• MIMIC-IV 2.1 ICU admissions are represented as sequences of 6 visits × 59 features.
• BiLSTM-attention, BiGRU, and Transformer models learn directly from visit sequences.
• Temporal feature engineering exposes trend, variability, velocity, and acceleration.
• Calibrated gradient boosting produces the strongest and most deployable model.

PROBLEM STATEMENT
2
• Single-visit classifiers discard clinically meaningful change across admissions.
• The target class is imbalanced: only 16.9% of samples represent worsening progression.
• Sequence models can rank risk well, but the baseline shows low precision at threshold 0.5.
• A useful system must preserve recall while reducing false-positive alerts.
• It must also be CPU-friendly, calibrated, and interpretable enough for deployment.
Research question: can explicit temporal descriptors outperform heavier sequence models?
